### Problem Statement
Question
A market research firm reports that 35% of small businesses use paid social media advertising. A marketing researcher randomly selects 300 small businesses. Find the probability that the sample proportion of small businesses that use paid social media advertising is between 32% and 40%. Show all steps using the SPDC structure.
Detailed Prompts

1.Define the population (true) parameter in context.
2.Define the sample parameter in context.
3.What is 300? What does it represent?
4.What is 35%? What does it represent?
5.Write the problem (the probability you are asked to find using p̂).
P – Plan
1.Check randomness: Was the sample random? Why does randomness matter?
2.Check independence (10% condition): How many small businesses must there be in the population? Is n < 10% of N?
3.Check normality (large counts condition): Is the expected number of successes at least 10? Is the expected number of failures at least 10?
4.Compute the mean of the sampling distribution of p̂.
5.Compute the standard deviation of the sampling distribution of p̂, and write the Normal model.
D – Do

2.Write the probability statement in terms of p̂.
3.Write the probability statement in terms of Z formula.
4.Convert both boundaries to z-scores.
5.Use the standard Normal table and write the value of the probability.
C – Conclude

### Model Solution
S – State.
1.Population parameter: p = the true proportion of all small businesses that use paid social media advertising.
2.Sample statistic: p̂ = the proportion of the 300 randomly selected small businesses that use paid social media advertising.
3.n = 300 is the sample size, the number of small businesses randomly selected by the marketing researcher.
4.35% is the population proportion, p = 0.35.
5.Problem: Find P(0.32 ≤ p̂ ≤ 0.40).
P – Plan.
6.Randomness: the 300 small businesses were randomly selected, so p̂ is an unbiased estimator of p and we can generalize to all small businesses.
7.10% condition: there must be at least 10(300) = 3,000 small businesses; there are far more than 3,000, so n < 10% of N and the observations are approximately independent.
8.Large counts: np = 300(0.35) = 105 ≥ 10 and n(1 − p) = 300(0.65) = 195 ≥ 10, so the sampling distribution of p̂ is approximately Normal.
9.Mean: μ_p̂ = p = 0.35.
10.Standard deviation: σ_p̂ = √[(0.35)(0.65)/300] ≈ 0.0275. Normal model: p̂ ~ N(0.35, 0.0275).
D – Do.
11.Sampling distribution: p̂ ~ N(0.35, 0.0275).
12.P(0.32 ≤ p̂ ≤ 0.40)
13.= P[(0.32 − 0.35)/0.0275 ≤ Z ≤ (0.40 − 0.35)/0.0275]
14.= P(−1.09 ≤ Z ≤ 1.82)
15.= 0.9656 − 0.1379 = 0.8277
C – Conclude.
•There is about an 83% probability that, in a random sample of 300 small businesses, the sample proportion that use paid social media advertising will be between 32% and 40%.
