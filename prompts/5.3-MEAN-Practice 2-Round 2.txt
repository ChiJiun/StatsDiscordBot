### Problem Statement
Question
A hotel chain reports that the length of stay per reservation has a mean of 2.8 nights and a standard deviation of 1.9 nights. The distribution of lengths of stay is skewed to the right. A revenue manager randomly selects 40 reservations. Find the probability that the mean length of stay in the sample is greater than 3.2 nights. Show all steps using the SPDC structure.
Detailed Prompts
S – State
1.Define the population (true) parameter in context.
2.Define the sample parameter in context.
3.What is 40? What does it represent?
4.What are 2.8 nights and 1.9 nights? What do they represent? What is the shape of the population distribution?
5.Write the problem (the probability you are asked to find using x̄).
P – Plan
1.Check randomness: Was the sample random? Why does randomness matter?
2.Check independence (10% condition): How many reservations must there be in the population? Is n < 10% of N?
3.Check normality: The population is skewed. Is the sample size large enough for the Central Limit Theorem (n ≥ 30)? What is the shape of the sampling distribution of x̄?
4.Compute the mean of the sampling distribution of x̄.
5.Compute the standard deviation of the sampling distribution of x̄, and write the Normal model.
D – Do
1.Write the sampling distribution of the sample statistic.
2.Write the probability statement in terms of x̄.
3.Write the probability statement in terms of Z formula.
4.Convert the boundary to a z-score.
5.Use the standard Normal table and write the value of the probability.
C – Conclude

### Model Solution
S – State.
1.Population parameter: μ = the true mean length of stay for all of the hotel chain's reservations.
2.Sample statistic: x̄ = the mean length of stay of the 40 randomly selected reservations.
3.n = 40 is the sample size, the number of reservations randomly selected by the revenue manager.
4.2.8 nights is the population mean (μ = 2.8) and 1.9 nights is the population standard deviation (σ = 1.9); the population distribution is skewed to the right.
5.Problem: Find P(x̄ > 3.2).
P – Plan.
6.Randomness: the 40 reservations were randomly selected, so x̄ is an unbiased estimator of μ and we can generalize to all the chain's reservations.
7.10% condition: there must be at least 10(40) = 400 reservations; the hotel chain has far more than 400 reservations, so n < 10% of N and the observations are approximately independent.
8.Normality: the population is skewed right, but n = 40 ≥ 30, so by the Central Limit Theorem the sampling distribution of x̄ is approximately Normal.
9.Mean: μ_x̄ = μ = 2.8.
10.Standard deviation: σ_x̄ = σ/√n = 1.9/√40 ≈ 0.300. Normal model: x̄ ~ N(2.8, 0.300).
D – Do.
11.Sampling distribution: x̄ ~ N(2.8, 0.300).
12.P(x̄ > 3.2)
13.= P[Z > (3.2 − 2.8)/0.300]
14.= P(Z > 1.33)
15.= 1 − 0.9082 = 0.0918
C – Conclude.
•There is about a 9% probability that, in a random sample of 40 of the hotel chain's reservations, the sample mean length of stay will be greater than 3.2 nights.
