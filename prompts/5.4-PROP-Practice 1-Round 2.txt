### Problem Statement
Question
A human resources research firm reports that 22% of employees at large companies plan to change jobs within the next year. An HR consultant randomly selects 500 employees at large companies. Find the probability that the sample proportion of employees who plan to change jobs is less than 20%. Show all steps using the SPDC structure.
Detailed Prompts

2. Define the sample parameter in context.
3. What is 500? What does it represent?
4. What is 22%? What does it represent?
5. Write the problem (the probability you are asked to find using p̂).
P – Plan
1. Check randomness: Was the sample random? Why does randomness matter?
2. Check independence (10% condition): How many employees at large companies must there be in the population? Is n < 10% of N?
3. Check normality (large counts condition): Is the expected number of successes at least 10? Is the expected number of failures at least 10?
4. Compute the mean of the sampling distribution of p̂.
5. Compute the standard deviation of the sampling distribution of p̂, and write the Normal model.
D – Do

2. Write the probability statement in terms of p̂.
3. Write the probability statement in terms of Z formula.
4. Convert the boundary to a z-score.

### Model Solution
S – State.
1. Population parameter: p = the true proportion of all employees at large companies who plan to change jobs within the next year.
2. Sample statistic: p̂ = the proportion of the 500 randomly selected employees who plan to change jobs within the next year.
3. n = 500 is the sample size, the number of employees at large companies randomly selected by the HR consultant.
4. 22% is the population proportion, p = 0.22.
5. Problem: Find P(p̂ < 0.20).
P – Plan.
1. Randomness: the 500 employees were randomly selected, so p̂ is an unbiased estimator of p and we can generalize to all employees at large companies.
2. 10% condition: there must be at least 10(500) = 5,000 employees at large companies; there are far more than 5,000, so n < 10% of N and the observations are approximately independent.
3. Large counts: np = 500(0.22) = 110 ≥ 10 and n(1 − p) = 500(0.78) = 390 ≥ 10, so the sampling distribution of p̂ is approximately Normal.
4. Mean: μ_p̂ = p = 0.22.
5. Standard deviation: σ_p̂ = √[(0.22)(0.78)/500] ≈ 0.0185. Normal model: p̂ ~ N(0.22, 0.0185).
D – Do.
1. Sampling distribution: p̂ ~ N(0.22, 0.0185).
2. P(p̂ < 0.20)
3. = P[Z < (0.20 − 0.22)/0.0185]
4. = P(Z < −1.08)
5. = 0.1401
C – Conclude.
• There is about a 14% probability that, in a random sample of 500 employees at large companies, the sample proportion who plan to change jobs within the next year will be less than 20%.
