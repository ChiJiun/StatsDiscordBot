### Problem Statement
A logistics company reports that 8% of its packages are delivered late. An operations manager randomly selects 600 packages shipped last month. Find the probability that the sample proportion of packages delivered late is greater than 10%. Show all steps using the SPDC structure.

### Model Solution
S – State.
1. Population parameter: p = the true proportion of all the company's packages that are delivered late.
2. Sample statistic: p̂ = the proportion of the 600 randomly selected packages that are delivered late.
3. n = 600 is the sample size, the number of packages randomly selected by the operations manager.
4. 8% is the population proportion, p = 0.08.
5. Problem: Find P(p̂ > 0.10).
P – Plan.
1. Randomness: the 600 packages were randomly selected, so p̂ is an unbiased estimator of p and we can generalize to all the company's packages.
2. 10% condition: there must be at least 10(600) = 6,000 packages; a logistics company ships far more than 6,000 packages, so n < 10% of N and the observations are approximately independent.
3. Large counts: np = 600(0.08) = 48 ≥ 10 and n(1 − p) = 600(0.92) = 552 ≥ 10, so the sampling distribution of p̂ is approximately Normal.
4. Mean: μ_p̂ = p = 0.08.
5. Standard deviation: σ_p̂ = √[(0.08)(0.92)/600] ≈ 0.0111. Normal model: p̂ ~ N(0.08, 0.0111).
D – Do.
1. Sampling distribution: p̂ ~ N(0.08, 0.0111).
2. P(p̂ > 0.10)
3. = P[Z > (0.10 − 0.08)/0.0111]
4. = P(Z > 1.80)
5. = 1 − 0.9641 = 0.0359
C – Conclude.
• There is about a 3.6% probability that, in a random sample of 600 of the company's packages, the sample proportion delivered late will be greater than 10%.
