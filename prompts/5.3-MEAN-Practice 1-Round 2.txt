### Problem Statement
Question
An online retailer reports that the time customers spend on its website per visit has a mean of 12.4 minutes and a standard deviation of 5.0 minutes. The distribution of visit times is skewed to the right. A marketing analyst randomly selects 50 visits. Find the probability that the mean visit time in the sample is between 11.5 and 13.5 minutes. Show all steps using the SPDC structure.
Detailed Prompts
S – State
1. Define the population (true) parameter in context.
2. Define the sample parameter in context.
3. What is 50? What does it represent?
4. What are 12.4 minutes and 5.0 minutes? What do they represent? What is the shape of the population distribution?
5. Write the problem (the probability you are asked to find using x̄).
P – Plan
1. Check randomness: Was the sample random? Why does randomness matter?
2. Check independence (10% condition): How many visits must there be in the population? Is n < 10% of N?
3. Check normality: The population is skewed. Is the sample size large enough for the Central Limit Theorem (n ≥ 30)? What is the shape of the sampling distribution of x̄?
4. Compute the mean of the sampling distribution of x̄.
5. Compute the standard deviation of the sampling distribution of x̄, and write the Normal model.
D – Do
1. Write the sampling distribution of the sample statistic.
2. Write the probability statement in terms of x̄.
3. Write the probability statement in terms of Z formula.
4. Convert both boundaries to z-scores.
5. Use the standard Normal table and write the value of the probability.
C – Conclude
1. Start your conclusion with "There is about a ___ probability that…"
2. What is the sample size, and what is in the sample?
3. What statistic does the sample produce?
4. What interval is in the question?
5. What does this mean describe in context?

### Model Solution
S – State.
1. Population parameter: μ = the true mean time per visit for all visits to the retailer's website.
2. Sample statistic: x̄ = the mean time per visit of the 50 randomly selected visits.
3. n = 50 is the sample size, the number of website visits randomly selected by the marketing analyst.
4. 12.4 minutes is the population mean (μ = 12.4) and 5.0 minutes is the population standard deviation (σ = 5.0); the population distribution is skewed to the right.
5. Problem: Find P(11.5 ≤ x̄ ≤ 13.5).
P – Plan.
6. Randomness: the 50 visits were randomly selected, so x̄ is an unbiased estimator of μ and we can generalize to all visits to the website.
7. 10% condition: there must be at least 10(50) = 500 visits; the website has far more than 500 visits, so n < 10% of N and the observations are approximately independent.
8. Normality: the population is skewed right, but n = 50 ≥ 30, so by the Central Limit Theorem the sampling distribution of x̄ is approximately Normal.
9. Mean: μ_x̄ = μ = 12.4.
10. Standard deviation: σ_x̄ = σ/√n = 5.0/√50 ≈ 0.707. Normal model: x̄ ~ N(12.4, 0.707).
D – Do.
11. Sampling distribution: x̄ ~ N(12.4, 0.707).
12. P(11.5 ≤ x̄ ≤ 13.5)
13. = P[(11.5 − 12.4)/0.707 ≤ Z ≤ (13.5 − 12.4)/0.707]
14. = P(−1.27 ≤ Z ≤ 1.56)
15. = 0.9406 − 0.1020 = 0.8386
C – Conclude.
• There is about an 84% probability that, in a random sample of 50 visits to the retailer's website, the sample mean visit time will be between 11.5 and 13.5 minutes.
